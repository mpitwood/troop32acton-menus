Leader / Counselor Answer Key — Real Food Check

This document exists so a leader or merit badge counselor can quickly check a scout's menu-planning worksheet (food-group sort, protein/vegetable/processed- ingredient identification, and nutrition reasoning) against what's actually in a recipe, without opening all 49+ recipe pages one at a time. It compiles each page's Real Food Check section — and, for backpacking, the opening weight/calorie/protein line — in one place.

This stays in leader/counselor hands during sign-off. Don't hand it to a scout before they've done their own worksheet — a scout should work from each recipe's Ingredients list directly (linked below) and sort/reason through it themselves, not copy this answer key.

Front-Country

Breakfast

Breakfast Burrito Bar — Vegetarian Base + Sausage Boost

  • Protein (base): black beans, cheese
  • Vegetables: sweet potato, bell pepper, onion
  • Processed: none in the base; sausage boost is a processed meat and optional — the base holds up as a complete dish without it

Camp Pancakes

  • Protein: eggs, milk — present but modest, not the anchor of the meal
  • Processed: boxed pancake mix, syrup (added sugar)
  • Pair with a protein-forward side (eggs, sausage) rather than serving pancakes and syrup alone

Dutch Oven Breakfast Casserole — Vegetarian Base + Sausage Boost

  • Protein (base): eggs, cheddar cheese
  • Vegetables: bell pepper, onion
  • Processed: frozen hash browns (convenience shortcut); sausage boost is a processed meat

Dutch Oven Monkey Bread

  • Protein: minimal — only what's in the butter and dough
  • Processed: refrigerated biscuit dough (main ingredient), a full cup of added sugar plus brown sugar
  • Treat, not a standalone breakfast — pair with fruit or a protein side

Loaded Breakfast Hash — Vegetarian Base + Bacon Boost

  • Protein (base): cheese (optional); + bacon (boost)
  • Vegetables: potato, onion, bell pepper
  • Processed: frozen diced potatoes (convenience — fresh potatoes swap in with zero recipe changes); bacon boost is a processed meat

Oatmeal Toppings Bar

  • Protein: milk (or yogurt cups), sunflower seeds
  • Whole food: oats, dried fruit, banana
  • Processed: none significant — brown sugar is the only added sugar, and it's a topping scouts add themselves rather than baked in

Lunch

Camp Pasta Salad

  • Protein: chickpeas, mozzarella
  • Vegetables: cherry tomatoes, cucumber, red onion
  • Processed: bottled dressing — swap in a homemade oil-and-vinegar mix for a fully whole-food version

Campfire Quesadillas

  • Protein: chicken, black beans, cheese
  • Vegetables: bell pepper, corn
  • Processed: tortillas are the one refined-grain item; no need to add more bread or chips on the side

Grilled Cheese & Tomato Soup — Vegetarian Base + Ham Boost

  • Protein (base): cheese; + ham (boost)
  • Vegetables: tomato (via soup)
  • Processed: canned tomato soup — a minimally processed pantry staple, not ultra-processed; blend crushed tomatoes with butter and broth instead for a fully whole-food version

Loaded Baked Potato Bar — Vegetarian Base + Bacon Boost

  • Protein (base): cheese, sour cream, black beans; + bacon (boost)
  • Vegetables: potato, green onion
  • Processed: none in the base; bacon boost is a processed meat

Loaded Wrap Bar

  • Protein: deli turkey/ham, hummus, cheese
  • Vegetables: lettuce, tomato (+ optional cucumber, bell pepper)
  • Processed: deli meat is a processed product; everything else is minimally processed

Ramen Noodle Upgrade Bowl

  • Protein: chicken or tofu; optional soft-boiled egg
  • Vegetables: snap peas/green beans, carrots, corn, green onion
  • Processed: instant ramen noodles and seasoning packet — using only half the packet cuts a meaningful share of the sodium and processed flavoring

Dinner

One-Pot Cajun Red Beans & Rice — Vegetarian Base + Andouille Boost

  • Protein (base): kidney beans; + andouille sausage (boost)
  • Vegetables: onion, bell pepper, celery
  • Processed: none in the base; andouille boost is a processed meat, not required for the dish to stand on its own

Dutch Oven Chili — Vegetarian Base + Meat Boost

  • Protein (base): black beans, kidney beans; + ground beef (boost)
  • Vegetables: sweet potato, onion, bell pepper, corn
  • Processed: none in the base

Dutch Oven Enchilada Bake — Vegetarian Base + Chicken Boost

  • Protein (base): black beans, cheese; + chicken (boost)
  • Vegetables: corn, onion
  • Processed: canned enchilada sauce — a minimally processed pantry item, not ultra-processed; no need to make it from scratch

Dutch Oven Southwest Pizza — Vegetarian Base + Pepperoni Boost

  • Protein (base): black beans, cheese; + pepperoni (boost)
  • Vegetables: corn, red onion, bell pepper
  • Processed: naan/flatbread is a refined grain, doing the normal job of a pizza crust; pepperoni boost is a processed meat, optional

Foil Packet Fajitas — Vegetarian Base + Chicken/Steak Boost

  • Protein (base): portobello mushrooms, black beans; + chicken or steak (boost)
  • Vegetables: bell pepper, onion
  • Processed: none

One-Pot Creamy White Bean Pasta — Vegetarian Base + Sausage Boost

  • Protein (base): cannellini beans, parmesan; + Italian sausage (boost)
  • Vegetables: spinach, garlic, sun-dried tomatoes
  • Processed: sun-dried tomatoes are a minimally processed pantry item, not ultra-processed; pasta is a refined grain — no need to add bread or more pasta on the side, the beans and greens are already carrying the meal

Sides & Appetizers

Campfire Coleslaw

  • Protein: none — pair with a protein-forward main
  • Vegetables: cabbage, carrot
  • Processed: none

Campfire Dutch Oven Nachos

  • Protein: black beans, cheese (+ optional ground beef or chicken)
  • Vegetables: tomato, green onion, jalapeño
  • Processed: tortilla chips — a vehicle for the beans/cheese/toppings, not the point of the dish

Campfire Queso Dip

  • Protein: cheese (+ chorizo/ground beef add-in, optional)
  • Vegetables: tomatoes and chilies (via canned Rotel) only
  • Processed: the most processed recipe in the repo — processed melting cheese is exactly what the current guidelines push away from. Swap in real shredded cheddar + a splash of milk for a less-processed version.

Dutch Oven Garlic Biscuits

  • Protein: minimal
  • Processed: refrigerated biscuit dough — a refined-flour convenience item
  • Fine as an occasional side; don't pair with another refined-carb-heavy side in the same meal if trying to keep that meal protein/vegetable- forward

Grilled Veggie & Corn Foil Packets

  • Protein: minimal — this is a vegetable side, not a protein source
  • Vegetables: corn, zucchini, bell pepper, red onion
  • Processed: none

Smoky Baked Beans — Vegetarian Base + Bacon Boost

  • Protein (base): great northern/navy beans; + bacon (boost)
  • Vegetables: onion
  • Processed: none in the base — doctored with real pantry ingredients (mustard, ketchup, brown sugar, paprika) rather than a pre-made product

Desserts

Campfire Banana Boats

  • Protein: minimal
  • Fruit: whole banana carries the dish
  • Processed: chocolate chips, marshmallows — a sugar-forward treat, but built on real produce rather than a boxed mix

Campfire Pie Iron Fruit Pies

  • Protein: minimal
  • Processed: canned pie filling and white sandwich bread are both processed convenience items — this is a treat, not a "real food" standard-bearer. It earns its place for the hands-on cooking experience.

Dutch Oven Apple Crisp

  • Protein: minimal
  • Fruit: whole apples
  • Processed: none — one of the more whole-food desserts in the repo; added sugar is present but there's no processed mix standing in for real ingredients

Dutch Oven Brownies

  • Protein: minimal — from the eggs only
  • Processed: boxed mix, meaningful added sugar — occasional dessert, not a template for the weekend's food quality
  • The egg-free applesauce swap is also the less-processed version; worth using even without an allergy in the patrol

Dutch Oven Peach Cobbler

  • Protein: minimal
  • Fruit: canned peaches (real fruit)
  • Processed: boxed cake mix, meaningful added sugar — an occasional treat, not a "real food" target dish

No-Bake Rice Krispie Treats

  • Protein: minimal
  • Processed: cereal and marshmallows, no way around it — a treat, not a food-group-checking dessert. Earns its spot for being fast on a night the cook crew needs a break, not for nutrition.

Snacks

Ants on a Log (Sunflower Seed Butter)

  • Protein: sunflower seed butter
  • Vegetables/fruit: celery, raisins
  • Processed: none

Campfire Kettle Popcorn

  • Protein: minimal
  • Whole food: popcorn kernels are a whole grain in its actual whole form, not milled/refined
  • Processed: none

Chilled Grape & Fruit Skewers

  • Protein: none — pair with a protein-forward meal earlier in the day
  • Fruit: grapes, melon, strawberries
  • Processed: none — whole fruit, nothing added

No-Bake Sunflower Seed Energy Bites

  • Protein: sunflower seed butter, oats
  • Processed: mini chocolate chips — a flavor accent in a small amount, not the point of the snack; everything else is whole or minimally processed

Nut-Free Snack Basket

  • Protein: sunflower seeds, cheese
  • Fruit: apples, grapes, dried cranberries/raisins
  • Processed: trail mix leans processed (chocolate/candy, pretzels); the fruit-and-cheese plate is straightforwardly whole-food

Veggie Dippers with Hummus

  • Protein: hummus (chickpeas, tahini)
  • Vegetables: carrots, bell pepper, cucumber
  • Processed: none

Backpacking

Breakfast

Backcountry Breakfast Burrito — Vegetarian Base + Sausage Boost

Nutrition: 5.2 oz/serving base + ~1.5 oz/meat-eater · ~536 kcal/serving base (103 kcal/oz) + ~240 kcal boost · ~31g protein base + ~13.5g boost

  • Protein (base): bean flakes, TVP, cheese powder; + dehydrated sausage (boost)
  • Vegetables: dehydrated bell pepper and onion
  • Processed: dehydrated bean flakes, TVP, and cheese powder are processed convenience items, standard for backpacking — there isn't a lighter whole-food substitute that still packs this small

Cold-Soak Muesli

Nutrition: 6.4 oz/serving · ~704-774 kcal/serving (110-121 kcal/oz) · ~28-31g protein

  • Protein: milk powder, chia seeds, sunflower seeds; + jerky or sunflower seed butter (protein side)
  • Whole food: rolled oats, dried fruit
  • Processed: none in the base — about as close to a whole-food breakfast as backpacking gets

Hot Oatmeal Bar (Trail)

Nutrition: 5.3 oz/serving · ~613-683 kcal/serving (116-129 kcal/oz) · ~24-27g protein

  • Protein: milk powder, sunflower seeds; + jerky or sunflower seed butter (protein side)
  • Whole food: oats, dried fruit
  • Processed: none significant in the base — brown sugar is the only added sugar, in a controlled small amount

No-Cook Bar & Protein Breakfast

Nutrition: 5.9 oz/serving · ~710-780 kcal/serving (120-132 kcal/oz) · ~17.5-20.5g protein

  • Protein: jerky or sunflower seed butter; modest amounts from the bar and granola
  • Processed: the bar, granola pouch, and drink mix are processed convenience items — standard for a zero-fuel trail breakfast, not something to eat exclusively for a multi-day trip
  • Pair with a hot breakfast (oatmeal bar, breakfast burrito) on days that don't need an early start, rather than using this every morning

Lunch

Cold-Soak Couscous Salad — Vegetarian Base + Salami Boost

Nutrition: 6.45 oz/serving base + ~1 oz/meat-eater · ~738 kcal/serving base (114 kcal/oz) + ~140 kcal boost · ~32g protein base + ~6g boost

  • Protein (base): TVP, parmesan powder, plus the bar; + pepperoni/ salami (boost)
  • Vegetables: dehydrated tomato/pepper flakes
  • Processed: couscous is a refined grain; parmesan powder, TVP, and the bar are processed — the TVP is specifically there to make sure the vegetarian base doesn't fall short on protein without the meat boost

Crackers & Spread Bar

Nutrition: 7.25 oz/serving · ~858-908 kcal/serving (118-125 kcal/oz) · ~32-35g protein

  • Protein: cheese spread, meat stick or sunflower seed butter, plus the bar
  • Processed: crackers, cheese spread wedges, and the bar are processed convenience items, standard for a no-cook trail lunch

Hummus Wrap — Vegetarian Base + Tuna/Salmon Boost

Nutrition: 6.5 oz/serving base + ~2.6 oz/meat-eater · ~705 kcal/serving base (108 kcal/oz) + ~90 kcal boost · ~23g protein base + ~13g boost

  • Protein (base): hummus (chickpeas, tahini), plus the bar; + tuna/ salmon (boost)
  • Vegetables: dehydrated veggie flakes
  • Processed: hummus powder is dehydrated, not heavily processed; tuna/salmon pouches are minimally processed

Trail Tortilla Wrap Bar

Nutrition: 7.75-8.5 oz/serving · ~840-950 kcal/serving (108-112 kcal/oz) · ~32-38g protein

  • Protein: cheese, pepperoni/salami or hummus powder, plus whatever the bar contributes
  • Vegetables: dehydrated tomato/pepper flakes, if using
  • Processed: pepperoni/salami and the bar are processed; hummus powder is a dehydrated whole-food product, less processed than it looks

Dinner

Backcountry Chili Mac — Vegetarian Base + Beef Boost

Nutrition: 6 oz/serving base + ~1 oz/meat-eater · ~645 kcal/serving base (108 kcal/oz) + ~110 kcal boost · ~34g protein base + ~15g boost

  • Protein (base): black beans, TVP, cheese powder; + ground beef (boost)
  • Vegetables: dehydrated bell pepper, onion, tomato powder
  • Processed: TVP and dehydrated flakes are processed convenience items standard for backpacking — there isn't a lighter whole-food substitute that packs this small for a multi-day trip

Backcountry Mac and Cheese — Vegetarian Base + Bacon Boost

Nutrition: 5.5 oz/serving base + ~1.25 oz/meat-eater · ~640 kcal/serving base (116 kcal/oz) + ~188 kcal boost · ~26g protein base + ~12.5g boost

  • Protein (base): cheese powder, pasta, milk powder; + bacon (boost)
  • Processed: cheese powder, milk powder, and butter powder are all processed convenience items — genuinely hard to avoid for a lightweight backpacking mac and cheese, this isn't a dish to hold to a whole-food standard

Backcountry Quesadilla Night — Vegetarian Base + Pepperoni Boost

Nutrition: 6 oz/serving base + ~1 oz/meat-eater · ~639 kcal/serving base (105 kcal/oz) + ~140 kcal boost · ~32g protein base + ~6g boost

  • Protein (base): black beans, TVP, cheese powder; + pepperoni (boost)
  • Vegetables: dehydrated bell pepper and onion
  • Processed: cheese powder, TVP, and pepperoni are processed; tortillas are a refined grain — beans and vegetables are still doing real nutritional work in the base

Lentil Couscous Curry

Nutrition: 6 oz/serving · ~650 kcal/serving (108 kcal/oz) · ~34g protein

  • Protein: red lentils and TVP carry this dish on their own — genuinely high protein and fiber for the weight, not a "needs meat to be real food" situation
  • Vegetables: dehydrated mixed vegetables
  • Processed: coconut milk powder and TVP are the most processed ingredients here, and both are doing real work (fat/calorie density and protein density for the weight) — a reasonable tradeoff for a backpacking dinner

Sunflower Butter Noodles — Vegetarian Base + Chicken Boost

Nutrition: 6 oz/serving base + ~1 oz/meat-eater · ~685 kcal/serving base (114 kcal/oz) + ~130 kcal boost · ~24g protein base + ~20g boost

  • Protein (base): sunflower seed butter, TVP; + chicken (boost)
  • Vegetables: dehydrated carrot and snap pea flakes
  • Processed: rice noodles are a refined-grain base — the sunflower seed butter, TVP, and vegetables are doing the real nutritional work here

Snacks

Protein Bar & Jerky Station

Nutrition: 3 oz/serving · ~350-380 kcal/serving (117-127 kcal/oz) · protein not stated on the page (varies by bar brand and protein side chosen)
