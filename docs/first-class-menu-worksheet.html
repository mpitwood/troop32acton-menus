First Class Menu Planning Worksheet — Requirement 2a

This is your worksheet, not a page from the recipe book. Requirement 2a asks you to plan a menu and explain how it meets nutritional needs — the recipe pages give you ingredients and instructions, but the food-group sorting and nutrition reasoning below has to be your own work. Don't copy answers from a page's "Real Food Check" section — read the Ingredients list yourself and fill this in from scratch. Bring the finished worksheet to whoever verifies this requirement for your troop (often a senior scout or your Scoutmaster, not a merit badge counselor — First Class doesn't use counselors).

Requirement 2a, as written: "Help plan a menu for one of the above campouts that includes at least one breakfast, one lunch, and one dinner, and that requires cooking at least two of the meals. Tell how the menu includes the foods from MyPlate or the current USDA nutritional model and how it meets nutritional needs for the planned activity or campout."

(A note on wording: this troop's recipe book uses RealFood.gov and the 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines, which replaced MyPlate in January 2026. Your Scoutmaster or the leader signing off may still say "MyPlate" — same idea, ask which term they want you to use.)

1. The campout

"One of the above campouts" means one of the outdoor overnight campouts you're counting toward First Class requirement 1a — not a hypothetical trip. Pick one you've actually been on or have booked.

  • Campout: ________________________ Date(s): ________________________
  • Trip type: ☐ Front-country (car camping) ☐ Backpacking
  • Nights: _____
  • Activity level: ☐ Easy / relaxed ☐ Moderate (day hikes, active program) ☐ Strenuous (long miles, elevation gain, cold weather)

Activity level matters in Section 5 — a strenuous backpacking day needs more food than a relaxed weekend in the campground.

2. Pick your three meals

Browse front-country-index.md (or backpacking/INDEX.md if this is a backpacking trip) and pick one breakfast, one lunch, and one dinner. Requirement 2a requires at least two of the three to require cooking — a page tagged "No-Cook" doesn't count toward that.

At least two rows above must be checked "Yes." If they're not, go back and swap a No-Cook page for a cooked one.

3. Check the menu fits everyone going

Before you go further, check your three picks against the actual patrol or crew going on this campout — not a hypothetical group.

  • List anyone going with a food allergy, intolerance, or diet (vegetarian, nut allergy, egg allergy, etc.): ________________________________________
  • For each meal, check the page's Diet/Allergen tags (Nut-Free, Egg-Free, Vegetarian/meat boost) against that list. Does every meal work for everyone as written, or does someone need a substitution called out?
  • If a substitution is needed, name it and where it happens (e.g. "Priya gets the vegetarian base, no meat boost added to her bowl"):

4. Sort the ingredients into food groups yourself

For each meal, open the page's Ingredients list and sort what's in it into the columns below. Write the actual ingredient, not just a checkmark — e.g. "black beans" not "✓".

Protein Vegetables/Fruit Grains Dairy Other/processed

Now look across all three meals together:

  • Is there a real protein source at every meal? ________________
  • Is there a vegetable or fruit at more than one meal? ________________
  • Anything in the "Other/processed" column doing real nutritional work anyway (e.g. cheese powder, TVP), vs. just there for taste? ________________

If this is a front-country trip: there's no calorie table to read off — answer these instead, in your own words:

  • Does each meal have a real protein source, not just a side dish?
  • Is there a vegetable or fruit at more than one meal?
  • Given the activity level you picked in Section 1, does this menu feel like enough food, too much, or too little? Why?

6. Explain it

Write 3–5 sentences, in your own words, explaining how this menu meets the nutritional needs of this specific campout. Use what you found in Sections 4 and 5 — don't just list ingredients again, explain why the combination works for this trip. This is what you'll tell the leader signing off on this requirement.

What comes next

This same menu carries forward into the rest of the Cooking section of First Class, so don't lose this worksheet:

  • 2b — build a budget and food-amount list from this menu, for feeding three or more youth, and secure the ingredients.
  • 2c — show which pans, utensils, and other gear this menu needs.
  • 2d — demonstrate safe handling/storage of perishables and proper disposal of camp garbage and waste water.
  • 2e — actually cook this menu on a campout: serve as cook, supervise your assistant(s), and supervise cleanup.
