Second Class Menu Planning Worksheet — Requirement 2e

This is your worksheet, not a page from the recipe book. Requirement 2e asks you to plan, cook, and explain one hot meal — the recipe pages give you ingredients and instructions, but the food-group sorting and nutrition explanation below has to be your own work. Don't copy answers from a page's "Real Food Check" section — read the Ingredients list yourself and fill this in from scratch. Bring the finished worksheet to whoever verifies this requirement for your troop (often a senior scout or your Scoutmaster, not a merit badge counselor — Second Class doesn't use counselors).

Requirement 2e, as written: "On one campout, plan and cook one hot breakfast or lunch, selecting foods from MyPlate or the current USDA nutritional model. Explain the importance of good nutrition. Demonstrate how to transport, store, and prepare the foods you selected."

(A note on wording: this troop's recipe book uses RealFood.gov and the 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines, which replaced MyPlate in January 2026. The leader signing off may still say "MyPlate" — same idea, ask which term they want you to use.)

5. Explain the importance of good nutrition

This part of the requirement isn't asking you to justify this one meal specifically — it's asking you to explain, in your own words, why good nutrition matters generally. A few prompts to get you started (you don't need to answer all of them, just write a real paragraph):

  • Why does a growing teenager need protein, and where did you get it in this meal?
  • Why do vegetables and fruit matter, even on a short weekend campout?
  • What happens to a scout's energy on a hike or busy camp day if they skip a real breakfast?

Your answer (3–5 sentences):

6. Demonstrate transport, storage, and prep

Requirement 2e specifically asks you to demonstrate this, not just describe it — so treat this as your rehearsal notes before you do it for real at camp.

  • Transport: How does each ingredient get from the store/pantry to camp without spoiling or getting crushed? (Cooler with ice for perishables, sealed containers for dry goods, etc.)
  • Storage: Once at camp, where does each ingredient live until cook time — cooler, dry bag, hung food storage? Anything that needs to stay cold the whole trip, not just during transport?
  • Prep: Read the page's Advance Prep (at home) and Onsite sections. What do you do before you leave home, and what happens at camp? ________________________________________________
  • Anything on this page that's a raw meat, egg, or dairy product needing extra care (keeping separate from ready-to-eat food, cooking to a safe temperature, washing hands/utensils after handling)? ________________
