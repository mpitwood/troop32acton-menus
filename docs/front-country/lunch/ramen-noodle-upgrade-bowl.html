Ramen Noodle Upgrade Bowl

Lunch · Main · Meat, Vegetarian, or Vegan · Nut-Free: Yes · Egg-Free: Yes (egg is an optional add-in) · 2-Burner Stove · Serves 6 · Prep 10 min · Cook 15 min

Instant ramen bricks, doctored up with real vegetables and a protein add-in so it isn't just a packet of noodles. Fast, cheap, and flexible enough for every diet in the patrol from one pot.

Ingredients (serves 6)

  • 6 packs instant ramen noodles (seasoning packets optional — using only half keeps sodium down)
  • 1 cup fresh snap peas or green beans, trimmed
  • 1 cup carrots, thinly sliced or shredded
  • 1 cup corn, canned and drained (shelf-stable — no cooler space needed)
  • 3 green onions, sliced
  • 2 cups cooked, sliced chicken or 1 (14 oz) block firm tofu, cubed (or both, cooked separately, for a mixed patrol)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil (optional)
  • Optional: soft-boiled eggs, one per scout who wants one

Shopping List

  • Instant ramen, 6 packs
  • Snap peas or green beans, 1 cup
  • Carrots, 1 cup
  • Corn, 1 can
  • Green onions, 1 bunch
  • Cooked chicken and/or firm tofu
  • Soy sauce
  • Sesame oil (optional)
  • Eggs (optional, for scouts without an egg allergy)

Equipment

  • 2-burner stove
  • Large pot (and a second small pot if making soft-boiled eggs)
  • Slotted spoon

Instructions

Advance Prep (at home)

  • Trim the snap peas/green beans and slice or shred the carrots — both hold up fine in a cooler for a day or two, unlike frozen vegetables, which either need to stay frozen (not realistic in a cooler) or get used the day they thaw.
  • Decant the soy sauce and sesame oil into small squeeze or dropper bottles instead of packing the full bottles for 1 tbsp and 1 tsp.
  • Canned corn needs no cooler space at all — pack it as a shelf-stable can alongside the ramen.

Onsite (at camp)

  1. Bring a pot of water to a boil. Add the snap peas/green beans and carrots, cook 2-3 minutes until just tender.
  2. Add ramen bricks (broken up) and the drained canned corn, cook per the noodle package time, usually 3 minutes.
  3. Stir in soy sauce and sesame oil (if using) and half the seasoning packets, to taste.
  4. Portion into bowls, top with sliced green onion and the protein add-in of choice (chicken or tofu).
  5. Optional egg: in a separate small pot, soft-boil eggs (6-7 minutes at a simmer), cool briefly in cold water, peel, halve, and offer as a topping only to scouts who want one — keeps the base dish egg-free for anyone with an allergy.

Real Food Check

  • Protein: chicken or tofu; optional soft-boiled egg
  • Vegetables: snap peas/green beans, carrots, corn, green onion
  • Processed: instant ramen noodles and seasoning packet — using only half the packet cuts a meaningful share of the sodium and processed flavoring

Allergy & Diet Notes

  • Nut-free: yes, as written (no peanut sauce).
  • Egg-free: yes — egg is an optional garnish only, never mixed into the shared pot.
  • Vegetarian/vegan: yes with tofu instead of chicken; check the ramen seasoning packet for fish/meat-derived ingredients, or skip the packet and season with soy sauce and sesame oil instead.

Cost Estimate

~$2.90/scout for 6: ramen ~$4, vegetables (snap peas/carrots/corn) ~$5, chicken/tofu ~$5.50 (tofu is cheaper, chicken pricier — this splits the difference), soy sauce/sesame oil ~$1, green onion ~$1.75, optional eggs ~$2. Boston-area pricing — see README.md for sourcing.

Scaling Notes

  • Patrol of 4: 4 ramen packs, scale vegetables to about 2/3.
  • Patrol of 8: 8 ramen packs, scale vegetables to about 1.3x — use the largest pot the patrol has, or cook in two batches so noodles don't clump.

Advancement Tie-In

  • Tenderfoot: assist in preparing this meal.
  • Second Class: counts as the hot lunch a scout cooks solo.
  • First Class: works as the lunch slot in a planned menu.
  • Cooking merit badge: satisfies the camp stove cooking method requirement.
