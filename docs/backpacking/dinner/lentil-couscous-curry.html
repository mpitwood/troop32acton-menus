Lentil Couscous Curry

Dinner · Main · Vegetarian/Vegan · Nut-Free: Yes · Egg-Free: Yes · Simmer (short) · Serves 1 (base) + optional boost · Weight: 6 oz/serving · ~650 kcal/serving (108 kcal/oz) · ~34g protein

This is the dinner that proves vegetarian doesn't need a meat boost to be a genuine main course — red lentils cook fast and pack real protein and fiber on their own, and a scoop of TVP closes the rest of the gap to a full dinner-sized protein target. A chicken boost is offered because choice matters, not because the base needs help.

Ingredients (per person)

  • 2.75 oz red lentils (fast-cooking, no soak needed)
  • 1.5 oz couscous
  • 0.75 oz coconut milk powder
  • 0.5 oz dehydrated mixed vegetables
  • 0.5 oz TVP (textured vegetable protein)
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • Salt

Optional chicken boost (sized to meat-eating scouts who want it):

  • 1 oz dehydrated or jerky-style chicken bits

Shopping List (for a patrol of 6)

  • Red lentils, ~16.5 oz
  • Couscous, ~9 oz
  • Coconut milk powder, ~4.5 oz
  • Dehydrated mixed vegetables, ~3 oz
  • TVP, ~3 oz
  • Curry powder
  • Optional: dehydrated chicken bits, for scouts who want the boost

Equipment

  • Pot, stove, lighter/matches

Instructions

Advance Prep (at home)

  • Portion the lentils, TVP, curry powder, coconut milk powder, and dehydrated vegetables into one bag per person per dinner day. Keep the couscous separate since it goes in later.

Onsite (at trail)

  1. Bring about 1.75 cups water per person to a boil. Add the lentils, TVP, curry powder, coconut milk powder, and dehydrated vegetables.
  2. Reduce to a low simmer, cover, and cook 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are tender.
  3. Stir in the couscous, remove from heat, cover, and let sit 5 minutes until the couscous absorbs the remaining liquid.
  4. Serve. Scouts who want the chicken boost stir it into their own bowl — the base is a complete meal without it.

Real Food Check

  • Protein: red lentils and TVP carry this dish on their own — genuinely high protein and fiber for the weight, not a "needs meat to be real food" situation
  • Vegetables: dehydrated mixed vegetables
  • Processed: coconut milk powder and TVP are the most processed ingredients here, and both are doing real work (fat/calorie density and protein density for the weight) — a reasonable tradeoff for a backpacking dinner

Allergy & Diet Notes

  • Nut-free: yes.
  • Egg-free: yes.
  • Vegan: yes, as written.

Packaging & Trash Notes

  • Pre-combine the lentil/TVP/spice/coconut-milk-powder portion into one bag; keep couscous in a second small bag since it's added at a different step — two bags per person per dinner, both packed out afterward.
  • Buy lentils, TVP, and coconut milk powder in bulk — all are common bulk-bin items even outside specialty outdoor retailers.

Cost Estimate

~$2.05/scout (lentils ~$0.55, couscous ~$0.30, coconut milk powder ~$0.68, dehydrated vegetables ~$0.50, TVP ~$0.18). Optional boost: ~$1.60 per scout who wants it (dehydrated chicken bits run higher per ounce than the beef/bacon boosts elsewhere in the repo). Boston-area pricing — see README.md for sourcing.

Scaling Notes

  • Patrol of 4/8: scales linearly.
  • Lentils, TVP, and couscous all keep indefinitely dry; this is a good "buy once for the whole trip" ingredient set.
